What RecallAware is — and isn't

RecallAware watches public government product-recall sources and notifies you when something matches what you've told it to watch for. It's a convenience and awareness tool, not a certified safety authority, and it does not replace checking a product's official recall notice directly from the issuing agency or manufacturer when a decision actually matters (returning an item, stopping use of it, seeking a refund).

RecallAware is currently in beta. Features, coverage, and behavior may change, break, or be removed without notice while it's in this stage.

Recall data — accuracy, timeliness, and completeness

Recall information shown in the app is pulled from public government sources (see the home page for the current list) and is not written, verified, or guaranteed by us. In particular:

  • Some sources have a known reporting lag — for example, one U.S. agency's own machine-readable feed has been confirmed to run roughly a week behind that same agency's public website. We surface data as soon as it's available from the source; we can't make a source publish faster than it does.
  • Coverage is not exhaustive. RecallAware currently monitors a specific set of countries and agencies (see the home page); a recall from a source not yet covered will not appear here.
  • A barcode lookup or category/hazard match reflects what's in our database at the time you check. A "not recalled" result means no match was found in the sources we monitor as of that check — it is not a certification that a product is safe, and it is not a guarantee that a recall won't be issued for it later.

RecallAware is provided as an early-warning and convenience tool, not a substitute for your own judgment or for checking directly with the issuing agency or manufacturer before acting on a safety decision.
